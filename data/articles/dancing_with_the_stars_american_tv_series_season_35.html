Season 34

Season thirty-five of Dancing with the Stars premiered on ABC and Disney+ on September 15, 2026,[1] and is scheduled to conclude on November 24, 2026.[2] This season was the fourth to air live on both networks simultaneously. Season nineteen champion Alfonso Ribeiro returned to host the installment, while former professional dancer and judge Julianne Hough returned as co-host.[3] A holiday television special will air following the season finale.[4]

Cast

Couples

On April 22, 2026, during an upfront for Hulu's unscripted programming, Ciara Miller and Maura Higgins were announced as the first celebrity participants for the thirty-fifth season.[5] Jackson Olson joined the cast three weeks later during an upfront for Disney's original programming.[6] On June 17, TMZ reported that Guillermo Rodriguez joined the cast;[7] Jimmy Kimmel confirmed Rodriguez's appearance later that day.[8] Throughout August, Tyler Cameron, Jenna Dewan,[9] Julia Stiles, Tatyana Ali,[10] Sarah Jane Nader and Conner Leavitt were named to the cast.[11] Nader is the younger sister of season thirty-three contestant Brooks Nader, and Leavitt is the husband of season thirty-four semi-finalist Whitney Leavitt.[12] Amber Glenn and Ezra Frech became the first Olympic and Paralympic champions to compete during the same season.[13]

The first celebrity-professional dancer pairing, Rodriguez and the reigning champion Witney Carson, was announced on Good Morning America on August 17, 2026.[14] At the time of the announcement, Carson was four months pregnant with her third child.[15] She became the first pro to enter an installment of Dancing with the Stars while pregnant,[16] and the first to compete that far into her second trimester.[15] Gleb Savchenko and Sasha Farber did not return as pros this season,[17] although Farber was still involved in other capacities.[18] After a five-year hiatus, Sharna Burgess returned to the lineup on August 24.[19] Adele Zaikman, winner of Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro, joined the series as a first-time pro on August 31.[20] She is the first Israeli professional dancer to be featured on the series.[21] Season thirty-four troupe member Hailey Bills was promoted to a first-time pro.[22] Her pairing with Nader marked the third same-sex partnership in the series' history, following JoJo Siwa and Jenna Johnson from season thirty and Shangela and Savchenko from season thirty-one.[23]

The full roster of celebrity participants and their partnerships was revealed on Good Morning America on September 2.[24] With sixteen couples, this season is tied with season nine and season thirty-one as having the largest cast in the show's history.[25]

Hosts and judges

The hosts and judges for the season were announced on Good Morning America on September 1, 2026, one day before the cast reveal.[35] Alfonso Ribeiro and Julianne Hough returned as hosts,[36] while Derek Hough, Carrie Ann Inaba, and Bruno Tonioli returned as judges.[36] Joe Buck served as the guest judge for Super Bowl Night.[37] Kym Johnson served as the guest judge for Dedication Night.[38] Shirley Ballas served as the guest judge for Horror Movie Night.[39] Cynthia Erivo served as the guest judge for Disney Night.[40]

The second season of the Dancing with the Stars podcast, hosted by season thirty-four quarter-finalist Andy Richter, launched on September 10.[41] An official Dancing with the Stars aftershow, hosted by season thirty-four champion Robert Irwin, aired live immediately after the second night premiere episode on September 16.[41]

Ritcher revealed the majority of the season's themes during the second episode of the Dancing with the Stars podcast.[42] He did not not reveal the theme for the fifth week of competition;[42] Super Bowl Night was announced on Good Morning America on September 30.[43] Throughout the season, Richter shared the songs and dance styles for the upcoming week.[44] The songs and dance styles for Dedication Night were exclusively revealed by People magazine on October 14.[45]

Dance troupe

Kamri Peterson, a former pro on Dancing with the Stars: Juniors, was announced as a first-time troupe member on August 2, 2026, during the series' inaugural fan convention.[46] Jaxon Willard, Carter Williams, and Onye Stevenson were announced as second-year troupe members on September 4.[47]

Week 1: Premiere

For the two-night season premiere, each couple performed one unlearned dance to a song that topped one of Billboard magazine's music charts during the year each celebrity was born.[53] The male celebrities performed on the first night and the female celebrities performed on the second night.[54] One couple was eliminated at the end of each night.[55]

Night 1 (Men)

The opening number, choreographed by Jamal Sims, was set to "Dancing on My Own" by Robyn.[56] The troupe performed a bumper to "Bangaranga" by Dara.[56] Due to technical difficulties, online voting for the night was excluded and only votes cast via text messaging were accepted.[57] The female celebrities and their professional partners performed a routine to "Cha Cha Cha" by Bruno Mars, choreographed by Brandon Armstrong and Jan Ravnik.[56] Adele Zaikman, Allen Genkin, Erik Linder, and Selena Hamilton from Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro performed a salsa bumper to "Dai Dai" by Shakira and Burna Boy.[56] Inspired by Paris Baguette, one of the season's sponsors, co-host Julianne Hough and Tessandra Chavez choreographed a number to "I Love Paris" by Tatiana Eva-Marie and the Avalon Band; Hough additionally performed the routine with the pros.[56]

Week 6: Dedication Night

Individual judges' scores in the charts below (given in parentheses) are listed in this order from left to right: Carrie Ann Inaba, Derek Hough, Kym Johnson, Bruno Tonioli.

Each couple performed one unlearned dance to honor the most influential figures and institutions in the celebrities' lives.[74] There was no elimination this week; all scores were carried over to the following week.[38] Two-time mirrorball champion Kym Johnson returned as a guest judge for the second consecutive year.[74] Val Chmerkovskiy was suspended from participating in this episode after he made inappropriate remarks on social media against judge Carrie Ann Inaba that referenced actor Will Smith slapping comedian Chris Rock during the 94th Academy Awards.[75] It marked the first time a professional dancer had been suspended in Dancing with the Stars history.[76] As a result, Jenna Dewan performed her Viennese waltz with Alan Bersten.[77]

For the second consecutive year, the friends, family members, and special guests selected by each celebrity contestant were incorporated into their routines.[74] For her Viennese waltz, Tatyana Ali was joined by her The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air (1990–1996) co-star Alfonso Ribeiro.[78] She also performed "(You Don't Know) How Glad I Am" by Nancy Wilson.[78] Maura Higgins was joined by professional Irish stepdancer Lochlan "Lucky" Howard for her paso doble.[79] Tyler Cameron was joined by his fiancée Tate Madden for his contemporary routine.[79] During the performance, the couple revealed that Madden was pregnant with their first child, a daughter.[79] At the end of her contemporary routine, Julia Stiles was joined by her 10 Things I Hate About You (1999) co-stars Joseph Gordon-Levitt, Larisa Oleynik, Andrew Keegan, Gabrielle Union, and Susan May Pratt.[80]

The troupe performed a bumper to "Stay (If You Wanna Dance)" by Myles Smith.[74] Hailey Bills and Brandon Armstrong performed a bumper to "Fever Dream" by Alex Warren.[74] Adele Zaikman and Allen Genkin honored Dolly Parton with a performance to "Here You Come Again" by Parton and Willa Amai.[74] Genkin, the eliminated pros, and the troupe performed a number to "CATastrophe" by Hilary Duff from The Cat in the Hat (2026).[74] It was choreographed by Bills and Bersten and featured an appearance from season thirty-two champion Xochitl Gomez.[79]
