Crystal Lake is an American slasher television series developed by Brad Caleb Kane that serves as the prequel to the Friday the 13th franchise, set seven years before the original film, in 1972.[3][4] Stars Linda Cardellini in the leading role of Pamela Voorhees, with Callum Vinson as a young Jason Voorhees, William Catlett, Gwendolyn Sundstrom, Cameron Scoggins, and Devin Kessler also starring.

Produced by A24, the series premiered on Peacock on October 15, 2026. It received generally positive reviews from critics.

Production

[edit]

Conception

[edit]

In September 2003, during a panel session at the Maniafest convention, Sean S. Cunningham spoke about the possibility of bringing Friday the 13th to television, with the series focusing on a group of teenagers living in the Crystal Lake area.[8] In October 2005, Cunningham discussed the potential series further. He explained that the idea was to call the series Crystal Lake Chronicles, and "set [it] in a town with all this Jason history". The series would focus more on "coming-of-age issues", in a similar style to Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Dawson's Creek, and Smallville, with Jason Voorhees as more of a recurring "background" character.[9]

In April 2014, Emmett/Furla/Oasis Films and Crystal Lake Entertainment planned to produce an hour-long Friday the 13th television series. The series was intended to focus on a group of characters at Crystal Lake, who have to deal with the return of Jason Voorhees, as well as discover new information about him and his family.[10] The series was being developed by the CW as of August 2015. Steve Mitchell and Craig Van Sickle were hired to write the plot, while Sean S. Cunningham, Randall Emmett, George Furla and Mark Canton were to be the executive producers,[11] but, one year later, the network decided to not move forward with the series. CW president Mark Pedowitz explained: "We had better pilots. The bottom line is we felt we had stronger things to go with, and we didn't go forward with it. It was well-written, it was darker than we wanted it to be, and we didn't believe it had sustainability... We didn't believe that it was a sustainable script, a sustainable series. It was a very good pilot, but not a sustainable series."[12]

Development

[edit]

In October 2022, a Friday the 13th prequel series titled Crystal Lake was announced to be in development by A24 with Bryan Fuller as showrunner. It was to be executive produced by Fuller, Victor Miller, Marc Toberoff and Rob Barsamian.[13] The following month, Fuller revealed to Fangoria that they had complete creative freedom of the franchise under the streaming rights. He further explained, "As a streaming series, we have the rights to do everything underneath the Friday the 13th umbrella. The movie rights are a completely different thing. They are tied up at New Line and are super, super messy and probably won't be untangled anytime soon, but as far as us chickens in the television industry, uh, roost, we have access to anything and everything that Friday the 13th has done up until this point."[14] One episode written by Fuller was described as "an hour-long chase" on a frozen Crystal Lake.[15]

Writing for the series was slated to begin in late January 2023, with Kevin Williamson set to write an episode for the first season.[16] It was reported that Adrienne King had been cast in a recurring undisclosed role. She previously portrayed Alice Hardy in the 1980 original film and the 1981 sequel, although ultimately King would not appear in the finished show.[16] Production was halted in May 2023 due to the 2023 Writers Guild of America strike.[17] In May 2024, it was revealed that Fuller had been fired from the series after A24 elected to take the series in a different direction.[18] Williamson also departed the project.[19] At one point in production, Charlize Theron was considered as the role of Jason's mother, Pamela Voorhees.[15] Filming was set to begin in August 2024, with a production budget of $10 million per episode for an eight episode season. Vincenzo Natali and Kimberly Peirce were set to direct episodes.[15]

Production

[edit]

Nick Antosca was in talks to join as showrunner.[15] In August 2024, it was announced that Brad Caleb Kane would serve as the series' new showrunner.[20] Michael Lennox directed the first through third episodes, Celine Held and Logan George directed the fourth through sixth, and Quyen Tran helmed the seventh and eighth.[21]

In March 2025, Linda Cardellini was cast as Pamela Voorhees.[22] In April 2025, Kane posted an image of an episode's production script and said that the series' production offices had opened,[23] and by May, interior sets were under construction.[24] William Catlett was cast as Levon Brooks.[25] In June 2025, Devin Kessler, Cameron Scoggins, and Gwendolyn Sundstrom were added to the main cast.[21] Callum Vinson plays a young Jason Voorhees, Pamela's son, in a recurring guest role.[7]

Under the shooting title Mama's Boy, principal photography began in New Jersey on June 20, 2025.[26] Scenes were shot in Jersey City and West Milford. Filming of interior and exterior shots at the Rutt's Hut in Clifton took place from August 1 to 8.[27] Production later moved to Camp No-Be-Bo-Sco in Blairstown, the same camp location and town used in the 1980 original film, Friday the 13th.[28] Filming concluded in October 2025.[29] Other locations were scattered over five counties in New Jersey: Camp Tamarack, the National Newark Building, Cinelease-Caven Point Studio, Lewis Morris Park, Christos M. Cotsakos College of Business and the Blairstown Museum.[30]

In April 2026, Kane shared on Instagram that all eight episodes were in the final stages of post-production, completing audio mixing.[31]

Music

[edit]

Crystal Lake was composed by Italian musician Giosuè Greco who scored the series with veteran series composer Harry Manfredini who performed both the series' main title theme and end credits theme only in the series finale episode.[32] The series soundtrack album titled Camp Songs, was available on A24 Music on October 30, 2026.[33]

Release

[edit]

Crystal Lake premiered on Peacock in the United States with all episodes on October 15, 2026.[34] It was released simultaneously on HBO Max in Europe, Africa and the Middle East, on Crave in Canada, and on October 19 on Sky Atlantic and Now in the United Kingdom and Ireland.[35][36][37][38] Following its release, the series debuted at number 1 in the U.S. and Canada, maintaining its number 1 spot for two consecutive weeks in Canada.[39][40]

The series had preview screenings in the Primetime program in September at the 2026 Toronto International Film Festival and at the New York Comic Con and Beyond Fest in October.[41][42][43]

Reception

[edit]

On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, 77% of 77 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 6.6/10. The website's consensus reads: "Linda Cardellini plays the perfect mom who can do no wrong in this slasher prequel that lives up to the best and worst of its franchise's ilk, but since mother knows best, Crystal Lake is a campy, gory, good time."[44] Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 61 out of 100 based on 22 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[45]

William Goodman of TheWrap praised Cardellini's performance in Crystal Lake, describing her as the strongest element of the Friday the 13th prequel. Goodman highlighted Cardellini's portrayal of Pamela Voorhees and the series' attempts to expand the franchise's mythology, while criticizing its difficulty balancing established canon with an original story.[46] Peter Gray of The AU Review praised Crystal Lake as an ambitious and entertaining revival of the Friday the 13th franchise.[47]

Benjamin Lee of The Guardian criticized the series for unnecessarily expanding the straightforward premise of Friday the 13th, describing the series as an overcomplicated and misguided attempt to add backstory to the franchise. He also criticized its excessive subplots and inconsistent tone, arguing that the story struggled to justify its eight-episode format, despite Cardellini's performance and the graphic violence.[48]

Novelization

[edit]

A novelization of the series, written by Tim Waggoner, was published by A24 on October 15, 2026.[49]
