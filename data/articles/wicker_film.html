Wicker is a 2026 romantic fantasy comedy-drama film written and directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson, based on the short story "The Wicker Husband" by Ursula Wills. The film stars Olivia Colman, Alexander Skarsgård, Elizabeth Debicki, Marli Siu, Nabhaan Rizwan, Richard E. Grant, and Peter Dinklage.

Wicker premiered at the 2026 Sundance Film Festival on January 24, 2026, and was released by Black Bear Pictures in the United States on October 23, 2026, and the United Kingdom on October 30, 2026. The film received positive reviews from critics.

Plot

[edit]

A solitary Fisherwoman lives in a hut on the outskirts of a village, whose people often mock and harass her for her spinster status and her fishy stench. At the wedding of the Tailor's Wife's Sister to the inn's Bottlewasher, he struggles to fasten a ritual collar around her neck. After the wedding, unmarried women ritually shake small baskets containing eggs, making them emit yellow smoke. The Fisherwoman's dropped egg shakes by itself and emits purple smoke, indicating that she will be the next to marry. The women of the village take this as a negative omen.

The Fisherwoman visits the Basket Weaver and commissions him to make her a husband. The Basket Weaver accepts the task and advises her to meet him at the chapel in one month's time. On the eve of the wedding, the Wicker Husband arrives in the village, asking for clothing, shoes and lodging to prepare for the next day's ceremony. The Fisherwoman and the Wicker Husband are wed (without him putting a collar on her), and they consummate their union. Their passionate lovemaking causes the wooden bedframe to collapse, and the Wicker Husband offers to repair it. Soon, he completes many tasks to improve the Fisherwoman's modest hut, such as gardening and repairing her roof.

Their marriage becomes the talk of the village. At the Butcher's wedding, their unique and romantic dancing becomes a spectacle, and the women are moved by the Wicker Husband's romantic overtures to his wife. The men of the village become leery of the Wicker Husband, as his kind demeanor contrasts with their often boorish behavior toward their own spouses. At the inn, the men chastise the Wicker Husband and question the motive behind his domestic labours. He answers that his wife's happiness makes him happy.

The men remain skeptical, save for the young Bottlewasher, who resolves to repair his own home's roof to please his wife. The gesture goes awry when the roof collapses and he falls into the house. The town's Doctor, aided by his son-in-law the Tailor and his daughters, attempts to heal the Bottlewasher, but his wounds become fatal. His young wife is bereft, and the Wicker Husband offers her a comforting embrace after the funeral. This gesture stokes the Fisherwoman's suspicions, planted by the Tailor's Wife, that her husband has been unfaithful. He implores her to trust him, as he does her.

When winter arrives, the Bottlewasher's widow visits the Basket Weaver and desperately asks him to make her a baby. Shortly after, the Tailor's Wife discovers the wicker child. She brings her sister to the Fisherwoman's hut, claiming the child is proof of the Wicker Husband's infidelity and threatening to burn it if the Fisherwoman and her husband do not claim the child. The Wicker Husband insists the child is not his, but seeks to protect it still. The Fisherwoman throws her lantern at the Wicker Husband, who runs burning into the woods. She is immediately wracked with guilt and grief, and becomes reclusive in her solitude once more.

In spring, the Fisherwoman discovers the wicker child in the woods and takes him into her care. Meanwhile, the Basket Weaver finds the charred but living Wicker Husband and brings him to his workshop to repair him. The Basket Weaver visits the Fisherwoman, explains his role in the child's creation, and helps to reconcile husband and wife. He then goes to the village market and announces his retirement, and one final creation: a Wicker Woman, to be given to the villager who presents him with the most generous retirement gift. The village is abuzz with this news, dreaming greedily of the Wicker Woman's value.

When the Basket Weaver returns with the Wicker Woman, veiled in the back of his wagon, the villagers shower him with luxurious gifts in hopes of winning their prize. The Tailor's Wife arrives with a sack full of coins, announcing she and her sister will leave the village to start again, leaving the Wicker Woman to assume her domestic role in the Tailor's home. However, the lecherous Butcher, who has trapped his young wife in the cellar, comes through with a considerably larger sum of money and wins the prize. The Basket Weaver departs with his haul of gifts, leaving the Wicker Woman behind, not before explaining she is made of a different material than the Wicker Husband, who is made of supple willow. Now unveiled, the Wicker Woman is revealed to be made of dangerous blackthorn, and her kiss and touch stab the butcher, causing him to call for help, but he is ignored by the surrounding villagers.

Her plan thwarted, the Tailor's Wife decides to stay, though the Tailor is disgusted with her attempt to leave. Meanwhile, the Basket Weaver drives his wagon full of gifts, carrying the Fisherwoman, the Wicker Husband, and the wicker child to a new life abroad.

Production

[edit]

Development

[edit]

Topic Studios and Tango Entertainment financed and produced the film with Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson as directors, from a screenplay they adapted from the short story "The Wicker Husband" by Ursula Wills. Producers include Ed Sinclair and Tom Carver from South of The River, David Michôd and Brad Zimmerman for Yoki, Inc. and Justin Lothrop and Brent Stiefel for Votiv, as well as Olivia Colman, who leads the cast.[4][5]

Casting

[edit]

Alongside Colman, Dev Patel was initially attached but was replaced by Alexander Skarsgård. The cast also includes Peter Dinklage and Elizabeth Debicki.[6]

Filming

[edit]

Principal photography was completed by September 2025 in Budapest.[7][8][6]

Release

[edit]

Wicker was premiered at the Eccles Theater in Park City, Utah, US, as part of the Sundance Film Festival on January 24, 2026.[9] In February 2026, Black Bear Pictures, which also handled international sales on the film, acquired North American distribution rights at the European Film Market.[10] It closed the 59th Sitges Film Festival on October 18, 2026.[11]

The film was released in cinemas in the United States on October 23, 2026.[12] The film was then released a week later in the United Kingdom on October 30, 2026.[13] The film was released in Aotearoa New Zealand in October 2026 as part of the British and Irish Film Festival. Roadshow Films released the film in Australia on November 5.[14]

Reception

[edit]

Box office

[edit]

In the United States and Canada, Wicker was released alongside Klara and the Sun, Clayface, Wildwood, and Ali G: Who Iz I?, and was projected to gross $6–8 million from 1,471 theaters in its opening weekend.[15] It ended up debuting to $5.7 million, finishing in fifth.[16]

Critical response

[edit]

On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, 94% of 196 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.6/10. The site's critics consensus reads: "Wicker is a charming fable that strikes the right balance between naughty and nice, drawing much of its appeal from deftly deployed salty humor, seamless creature effects, and Olivia Colman's eminently lovable performance."[17] Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned the film a score of 70 out of 100, based on 39 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[18] Audiences polled by PostTrak gave the film a 92% positive score, with 72% saying they would definitely recommend it.[16]
