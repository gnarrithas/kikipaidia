VisionQuest is an American television miniseries created by Terry Matalas for the streaming service Disney+, based on Marvel Comics featuring the character Vision. A spin-off from and sequel to the miniseries WandaVision (2021), it is the 18th television series in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU) from Marvel Studios and is produced under its Marvel Television label. The series shares continuity with the films of the MCU. It follows the synthezoid Vision as he attempts to understand who he is by communicating with AI personas embedded in his programming, and goes on the run with a mysterious teenager who may be his reincarnated son. Matalas serves as showrunner and a director.

Paul Bettany reprises his role as Vision from previous MCU media, starring alongside James Spader, Ruaridh Mollica, Todd Stashwick, Lauren Morais, Orla Brady, Diane Morgan, Polly Frame, Henry Lewis, Jonathan Sayer, Emily Hampshire, James D'Arcy, Ben Crompton, T'Nia Miller, Art Malik, Mary McDonnell, and Joe Locke. Development of a Vision series began by October 2022, with WandaVision's Jac Schaeffer as head writer. She was no longer involved by May 2024, when Matalas was redeveloping the series and Bettany was confirmed to be starring. It was described as the final installment in a trilogy that began with WandaVision and continued with Agatha All Along (2024). Casting for the AI personas included some returning MCU characters and actors. Filming began by March 2025 and wrapped in July, taking place at Pinewood Studios in London with Christopher J. Byrne, Gandja Monteiro, Vincenzo Natali, and Matalas directing episodes.

VisionQuest premiered with its first two episodes on Disney+ on October 13, 2026, and ran for eight episodes, concluding on November 10. It is part of Phase Six of the MCU.

Premise

[edit]

After being resurrected in WandaVision (2021), the synthezoid Vision lives in hiding while he attempts to understand who he is by communicating with various AI personas that are embedded in his programming. When a bounty is placed on Vision's head by a mysterious person, he goes on the run with a mysterious teenager who may be his reincarnated son.[1]

Cast and characters

[edit]
  • Paul Bettany as:
    • Vision: A superpowered synthezoid and former Avenger created using the AIs J.A.R.V.I.S. and Ultron as well as the Mind Stone. Vision was killed in the film Avengers: Infinity War (2018) and then resurrected, with an all-white appearance and without his former memories or emotions, in the series WandaVision (2021).[2][3] After his memories were restored at the end of that series, Vision is now struggling to reconnect with those memories because he has no emotional attachment to them,[4][5] with showrunner Terry Matalas calling him a "blank slate";[6] he compared this journey to that of the Star Trek character Spock in the film Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986).[7] By the start of VisionQuest, Vision has been posing as a human for years,[8] and is now working at a pub under the name "Sherwood Ellis".[9][10] To cope with his identity crisis, he retreats into his internal Mindspace, where various AI personas embedded in his programming manifest through human avatars.[11]
    • The voice of Proto-One: An AI prototype created by Jo Carrington and her colleagues, which would serve as a template for J.A.R.V.I.S.[12]
    • Phineas Horton: A 20th century scientist who created J.I.M.,[13] with the purpose of having him replace his late son James. Horton sold his family estate in England to fund J.I.M.'s creation, which led him to neglect his wife and daughter—ultimately causing them to leave him and move to America.[14] By the 21st century, Horton is regarded as the "great-grandfather of robotics" for his creation of J.I.M.[15]
    • J.I.M. / Synthetic Man: The world's first synthezoid,[13] built by Horton in 1939 and modeled after his own image; Vision's human avatar is based on J.I.M.[14][16] He had a malfunction stemming from his "inability to process complex emotions" that caused him to undergo spontaneous combustion, resulting in both Horton's death and his own.[14] Horton's original source code for J.I.M. became the foundation for all the AIs that followed.[16]
  • James Spader as:
    • Ultron: An AI created by Tony Stark and Bruce Banner for a peacekeeping program, before developing a god complex and attempting to eradicate humanity. Since his android body was destroyed at the end of the film Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015),[17] Ultron has been uploaded by Vision into the Mindspace. There, he lives in a loft-like prison where he has access to the internet and spends his time painting,[18][19] which has given him a better perspective on humanity than Vision does;[20] Spader stated that Ultron is "probably the most human" of all the AIs in VisionQuest.[21] Matalas said the interactions between Vision and Ultron were a central dynamic for the series,[22] with Spader declaring that Ultron both loves and hates Vision,[19] and that the two characters have a "sort of father-son-therapist-psychopath" relationship.[23] Spader explored how Ultron had become a more humane AI since Age of Ultron, retaining a conflicted relationship with humanity but developing a genuine understanding of it.[24] Spader said VisionQuest allowed for further exploration of Ultron compared to his role in Age of Ultron, calling him one of the series' "driving forces" alongside Vision and Tommy Maximoff.[25]
    • Virgil Horton: Carrington's ex-husband/former business partner and the grandson of Phineas Horton. He was Paladin's client, placing a bounty on Vision's head.[26][27] He is also the human avatar for Ultron in the Mindspace.[28]
  • Ruaridh Mollica as Tommy Maximoff and Thomas Shepherd:
    Tommy Maximoff is the speedster son of Vision and Wanda Maximoff. In the series Agatha All Along (2024), his twin brother Billy Maximoff placed Tommy's soul into the body of Thomas Shepherd, a teenager who drowned.[29][30][31] In VisionQuest, Tommy is an 18-year-old orphan who is newly out of the foster care system in London,[32]: 4  and has been pickpocketing to survive, with Mollica stating that the character "likes to read and play people. There's a bit of Artful Dodgery."[8] Tommy experiences visions stemming from his memories of Wanda's Hex in WandaVision, which leads him to face an identity crisis as he struggles to reconcile the personas of Tommy Maximoff and Thomas Shepherd.[33][34] Mollica described Tommy as a grifter and small-time thief who is "lost in his life" and searching for a purpose,[35] noting that the character has "had to build who he is by himself, and there's a loneliness to that, and a yearning for family".[34] Mollica compared Tommy's journey of discovering his powers for the first time to that of Tobey Maguire's Peter Parker / Spider-Man in Sam Raimi's film Spider-Man (2002).[36] Mollica drew inspiration from Matt Damon's performance as Will Hunting in the film Good Will Hunting (1997) for Tommy's portrayal.[8]
  • Todd Stashwick as Paul Denning / Paladin:
    A bounty hunter with a mysterious past who is hunting Vision.[7][32]: 6  Trained in all forms of martial arts and weaponry, he is the latest in a "centuries-long line" of bounty hunters who have held the title of "Paladin",[37] which he was adopted into. Paladin's wife died after being struck by a bullet intended for him; furthermore, he has not seen his now-adult daughter for years, as he was one of the victims of the Blip and chose to keep his distance to ensure her safety.[38][39] He wears an adamantium chest armor provided by the Ten Rings Syndicate.[40] Matalas compared Paladin to the Star Wars bounty hunter Boba Fett, while Stashwick described Paladin as a badass and said he was similar to previous roles that Matalas had written for him.[41] Matalas drew inspiration from Tommy Lee Jones's performance as Sam Gerard in the film The Fugitive (1993) for Paladin's morally ambiguous personality.[8] Eli Gray portrays a young Paladin.[citation needed]
  • Lauren Morais as Lisa Molinari:
    A pickpocket and Tommy's partner-in-crime, with whom she develops romantic chemistry.[8][42] Mollica described Lisa as the only family Tommy has ever known,[43] as the two characters met on a prisoner transport vehicle.[8]
  • Orla Brady as F.R.I.D.A.Y.:
    An AI created by Tony to replace J.A.R.V.I.S. as his personal assistant.[7] She assists Vision with socialization issues, humor, and tactical ops,[44] and is based on Alison Hogan, the secretary to Tony's father Howard Stark, who helped raise Tony; Hogan is the human avatar for F.R.I.D.A.Y. in the Mindspace.[40][16] The series explores Tony's death in the film Avengers: Endgame (2019) from F.R.I.D.A.Y.'s perspective, with Matalas noting that the character was unable to protect the one person she had been programmed to protect.[45]
  • Diane Morgan as Tactical A.I. / Handler:
    Paladin's AI who operates from a simulation of a cramped, outdated "office" generated by a faulty server in Tajikistan.[46] Stashwick described the Handler as the closest thing Paladin has to a friend, stating that the relationship between them is a "cracked-mirror version" of the one between Vision and E.D.I.T.H.[38]
  • Polly Frame as Fiona Guthrie: The owner of The Old Selkie, a traditional Scottish pub in Edinburgh where Vision works[10]
  • Henry Lewis as D.U.M.-E.:
    A robot created by Tony to assist him in his workshop.[47] In the Mindspace, D.U.M.-E.'s human avatar is presented as a handyman, handling general maintenance and fire suppression.[48]
  • Jonathan Sayer as U.:
    A robot created by Tony to assist him in his workshop.[47] In the Mindspace, U.'s human avatar is presented as a handyman, handling general maintenance and acting as a defragger and video analyzer.[48] Sayer stated that D.U.M.-E. along with U. function like twins who are "super in sync", and added that both characters want to show the other AIs in the Mindspace that they are "equal and able to mix in with them".[8]
  • Emily Hampshire as E.D.I.T.H.:
    An AI created by Tony that ran an augmented reality security and defense system. After Tony's death, control of E.D.I.T.H. was briefly bequeathed to his protégé Peter Parker / Spider-Man in the film Spider-Man: Far From Home (2019).[49] Since then, E.D.I.T.H. has been "reprogrammed for fun",[32]: 5  shifting to handle recreation, game theory, and weaponry in the Mindspace.[44] She is based on Edith Marks, a fighter pilot and Howard's personal crash test dummy whose husband, Edward Marks, died in a plane crash; Edith serves as the human avatar for E.D.I.T.H. in the Mindspace.[50][51] Hampshire described the character as "basically the embodiment of all the human thrill-seeking joy in life", as well as "part cheerleader, part AI, part malfunctioning theme park ride".[32]: 5 
  • James D'Arcy as J.A.R.V.I.S.:
    The first AI created by Tony, developed to serve as his personal assistant before being used to create Vision in Age of Ultron.[52] In the Mindspace, J.A.R.V.I.S.'s human avatar is a butler resembling Edwin Jarvis, who served as the inspiration for his creation,[53][48] and he acts as an older-brother figure to Vision;[54] J.A.R.V.I.S. oversees security, housekeeping, and encryption in the Mindspace.[44]
  • Ben Crompton as Clancy: A guard on a prison bus that attempted to transport Tommy and Lisa to Saint Mabyn's Psychiatric Sanitarium[55]
  • T'Nia Miller as Josephine "Jo" Carrington / Jocasta:
    A neuroscientist who co-led a five-person team responsible for developing the first stable synthetic consciousness at the company they founded together, Markridge Biotech. Before dying in 2008, Carrington transferred her mind into the body of the synthezoid Jocasta;[56][57][needs update] in this form, she is cunning, powerful, and driven by revenge,[58] targeting the other four members of her former team under the guise of the serial killer known as the "Butcherbird Killer".[59] Miller described the character as a badass.[60]
  • Art Malik as Simon Moorhouse: A researcher of Markridge Biotech[61]
  • Mary McDonnell as Katerina Wells: The CEO of Markridge Biotech[56][62]
  • Joe Locke as Billy Maximoff: Wanda and Vision's sorcerer son and Tommy's younger twin brother whose soul inhabited William Kaplan's body after his death in Agatha All Along.[63]

Additional actors in the series include Faran Tahir reprising his MCU role as Raza al-Wazar, the leader of the Ten Rings faction that kidnapped Stark in the film Iron Man (2008);[64] Charlie Russell as Violette, Horton's wife; and Dulcie Connor as Victoria, Horton's daughter and Virgil's mother.[65] Also appearing in the series are Bettany's wife Jennifer Connelly as the voice of the AI D.E.E.D.E.E.,[66] and Brian Quinn as Dale.[67] Matalas makes a cameo appearance as an interviewer for the channel FXN News,[68] and the character Watcher makes a non-speaking cameo in the series.[69] Robert Downey Jr. and Elizabeth Olsen make uncredited cameo appearances in a mid-credits scene in the final episode as Victor von Doom / Doctor Doom and Wanda Maximoff / Scarlet Witch, respectively.[70]

Production

[edit]

Background

[edit]

In March 2009, Marvel Studios hired writers to help come up with creative ways to launch its lesser-known properties, including the Marvel Comics character Vision.[71] Paul Bettany was cast as the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU) incarnation of Vision for the film Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015). This version of the character is a synthezoid who is partially created from J.A.R.V.I.S., an AI assistant voiced by Bettany in earlier MCU films. Following Vision's death in the film Avengers: Infinity War (2018), Bettany was revealed to be portraying a reassembled and reactivated version of the character in the Disney+ series WandaVision (2021). This version was referred to as "White Vision" or simply "The Vision",[72][73][74] and has an all-white appearance, similar to when the comic book character was resurrected with an all-white body and no memories or emotions.[72]

Jac Schaeffer, the head writer of WandaVision, signed a three-year overall television deal with Marvel Studios in May 2021 to develop additional Disney+ projects.[75] By October 2022, they were developing a series called Vision Quest,[76][77] which would center on Bettany's "White Vision".[77] Schaeffer began discussing Vision Quest during the development of another WandaVision spin-off series, Agatha All Along (2024),[78] and she was set as head writer for Vision Quest while working on that series.[77] Development of a Vision-centric series was rumored as early as March 2021.[79] The name "Vision Quest" was previously used for a comic book storyline in West Coast Avengers vol. 2 #42–45 (1988–1989);[76][80] it was unclear if the series would be a direct adaptation of that comic.[76] A writers' room was set to be formed in early November 2022,[76][77] to include Peter Cameron, Megan McDonnell, and Eileen Shim.[81][82] Cameron and McDonnell worked on WandaVision and Agatha All Along.[81] Vision Quest was expected to follow White Vision as he attempts to regain his memories and humanity, with potential for Elizabeth Olsen to reprise her MCU role as Vision's wife Wanda Maximoff / Scarlet Witch.[77]

Development

[edit]

By October 2023, Marvel Studios shifted its creative philosophy to a more traditional television development process, moving away from head writers and beginning to hire dedicated showrunners for its series.[83] By May 2024, Schaeffer was no longer developing Vision Quest due to her focus on making Agatha All Along,[2] as well as for "personal reasons" according to Bettany,[5] with Terry Matalas joining to redevelop the series and serve as its showrunner. Vision Quest was the first live-action series to be greenlit by the studio in nearly two years, as well as the first to move forward under that new development approach.[2] Marvel Studios hired Matalas after being impressed with his work on the third season of Star Trek: Picard (2023), with Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige being a particular fan of the Star Trek franchise.[2][84] Matalas was also set to direct for Vision Quest, alongside Christopher J. Byrne, Vincenzo Natali, and Agatha All Along episodic director Gandja Monteiro.[85]

Matalas met with Schaeffer upon joining the project, and Marvel Studios executive Brad Winderbaum said in September 2024 that it would be a "love letter" to the work Schaeffer had done, while being unique to Matalas.[86] The series was still being referred to by Marvel as Vision Quest,[87][88][64] but The Hollywood Reporter said this was not its official title.[17][89] Disney was referring to the series as simply Vision by May 2025,[90] before the title was officially announced as VisionQuest that October. The series has been described as the final installment of the trilogy that began with WandaVision and continued with Agatha All Along.[52] Describing the relationship to those other two series, Matalas said VisionQuest was more of a sequel to WandaVision, while touching on some elements from Agatha All Along.[91] Marvel Studios's Mary Livanos, who worked on those two series, returned as an executive producer for VisionQuest,[87] alongside Bettany, Schaeffer, and Byrne,[92] while Roopesh Parekh was a producer.[93] The series is being released under Marvel Studios' "Marvel Television" label.[2]

Writing

[edit]

A writers' room opened during the week of May 20, 2024,[94] featuring Cindy Appel, Michael Taylor, Christopher Monfette, Nicole Falsetti, and Matthew Okumura, alongside Matalas.[95] Matalas described VisionQuest as an adventure and mystery drama, while also having elements of surreal comedy. The basis of the series was inspired by two scenes: the final conversation between Vision and his creator, the villainous AI Ultron, in Age of Ultron; and the "Ship of Theseus" discussion between the two versions of Vision in WandaVision.[91] Matalas stated that the former scene was the "launching point" for VisionQuest, while Bettany described the series as a "mash-up of the Pinocchio and Frankenstein stories".[3] Matalas said that he was given a great deal of freedom regarding the story of VisionQuest,[96] though Feige had requested that Vision be reunited with his son Tommy Maximoff in the series.[97]

VisionQuest is set around 2028, 18 months after Agatha All Along and years after the events of WandaVision,[98][99] at the end of which Vision's memories were restored.[4] It focuses on the character's struggle to reconnect with those memories because he has no emotional attachment to them,[29][5] which leads him to go through an identity crisis;[100][101] Emily Hampshire stated that the "whole show is really about identity".[102] As part of his attempts to understand who he is, Vision interacts with the human population disguised as one of them during the day, while at night he retreats into the quantum processors of his own mind, where he has created his own Mindspace—which manifests as a peculiar, gleaming white mansion, filled with environments that he can alter at will.[5][103] There, he communicates with various AI personas he rescued from the Avengers Compound and which are embedded in his programming.[20] Vision urges all these AIs to assume human avatars, just as he does,[104][105] and they possess distinct characteristics that help him understand different human qualities and abilities.[100][5] Bettany said the AI personas are inside the Mindspace because Vision had "been saving and copying and pasting them to keep them alive", including Ultron, whom Bettany stated "has to be kept behind a pretty impressive firewall because he's a psychopath";[106] this firewall keeps him locked away in the mansion's attic.[103]

Ultron returns in VisionQuest following his apparent death in Age of Ultron because the creative team wanted to include a character who was "really strong for Vision to play off of", and since they did not have access to Wanda, Ultron was chosen instead.[23] Matalas stated that the goal of the series was to have Vision and Ultron "dissecting humanity and what it means to be a father", noting that both characters "deal with connection": Vision has become disconnected from the love he once felt, while Ultron finds a connection of his own,[107] in the synthezoid Jocasta,[108] who, in the comics, was created by Ultron to be his bride, before she rejected him.[109] Bettany said that the story of VisionQuest revolves largely around the relationship between Vision and Ultron,[110] as the two characters share a complex dynamic that Bettany was eager to explore.[102] Despite Wanda not appearing in the series,[111] Matalas confirmed that Vision is aware of her death in the MCU film Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022).[23]

The series continues the WandaVision trilogy theme of exploring parenthood, specifically fatherhood. Winderbaum said VisionQuest was about three generations—a grandfather (Ultron), father (Vision), and son (Tommy)—and would explore if a man who had an abusive father could be a good father himself.[112][5] Bettany said VisionQuest explores intergenerational trauma with fathers and sons, similar to how WandaVision explores grief, as well as "denial of pain, and denial of your own truth, and coming to terms with who and what you are".[113] Another synthezoid introduced in VisionQuest is J.I.M. / Synthetic Man,[114] whose body was used by Ultron to create Vision in the comics.[115] Matalas noted that he was particularly interested in the complex father-son relationship between J.I.M. and his creator Phineas Horton—a dynamic that echoes the relationships Vision has with both Ultron and Tommy. Matalas added that he spent more time writing and rewriting the conversation between Vision, J.I.M., and Horton than any other scene in the series.[114] J.I.M. is not referred to by his original codename of "Human Torch" from the comics in VisionQuest because, at the time the series was in development, the MCU film The Fantastic Four: First Steps (2025) had not yet been released, and Matalas did not want the audience to confuse the character with Johnny Storm, who also goes by the name Human Torch and appears in that film, portrayed by Joseph Quinn.[13]

Matalas said each episode of VisionQuest feels like a different kind of film, similar to how each episode of WandaVision pays homage to a different era of American sitcoms.[116] Winderbaum said Matalas "knows the lore" of the MCU and that VisionQuest would be rewarding for fans who have followed the franchise for years.[29] Matalas described one scene in the series finale as "a Marvel dream".[22] An unused scene he wrote featured Vision and Ultron in a church, discussing the human soul and its meaning—with Matalas noting that "AI reflecting on humanity is your way into so many different kinds of stories" that the series did not get to explore.[107]

Casting

[edit]
Paul Bettany and James Spader reprise their MCU roles as Vision and Ultron, respectively.

Bettany was confirmed to be reprising his MCU role of Vision with the news of the series' redevelopment in May 2024.[2] In August, James Spader was set to reprise his role as Ultron from Age of Ultron.[17] The character also appears as a human in VisionQuest because Matalas did not want him to look like a "CGI robot 24/7" incapable of expressing emotions or doing the things Spader can do with "just the twitch of an eyebrow".[104] Todd Stashwick—who previously worked with Spader on the television series Boston Legal (2004–2008), as well as with Matalas on the series 12 Monkeys (2015–2018) and Picard—joined the cast a month later.[89][117]

In January 2025, Faran Tahir was revealed to be reprising his role as Raza al-Wazar from the first MCU film, Iron Man (2008).[64] Following a five-month audition process,[118] Ruaridh Mollica joined the cast in February as a character referred to as "Tucker",[119] and T'Nia Miller was cast in the leading role of Josephine "Jo" Carrington / Jocasta in May.[58][56] Soon after, Hampshire was revealed to be portraying the key role of E.D.I.T.H.,[49] an AI introduced in the MCU film Spider-Man: Far From Home (2019) where she was voiced by assistant editor Dawn Michelle King.[120] Matalas wrote the role with Hampshire in mind, as they had previously worked together on 12 Monkeys.[8] Matalas revealed in August that Stashwick was playing the comic character Paul Denning / Paladin, and that Picard star Orla Brady was portraying the AI F.R.I.D.A.Y., replacing Kerry Condon who voiced the character in several MCU projects.[7] Mary McDonnell was revealed as part of the cast the next month,[121] playing Katerina Wells.[56]

In October 2025, several more cast members were revealed: Henry Lewis and Jonathan Sayer as D.U.M.-E. and U., respectively, two robots seen in the MCU films;[47] and James D'Arcy as J.A.R.V.I.S., the AI originally voiced by Bettany in the MCU films. D'Arcy portrayed Edwin Jarvis, the human inspiration for the AI J.A.R.V.I.S., in several MCU projects.[52][120] Matalas hired Lewis and Sayer after seeing them perform in the play Peter Pan Goes Wrong.[8][32]: 6  That same month, Mollica was revealed to be portraying Tommy Maximoff and Thomas Shepherd, taking over from Jett Klyne who portrayed Tommy in WandaVision and the MCU film Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022).[29][52] Mollica previously auditioned for the MCU role of Billy Maximoff, Tommy's twin brother.[122] In November, Lauren Morais and Diane Morgan were respectively revealed to be portraying Lisa Molinari and the Handler, the latter an AI associated with Paladin.[42][46]

The following month, in addition to Vision, Bettany was revealed to also be playing both Phineas Horton and J.I.M. / Synthetic Man in the series,[13] after the latter made a non-speaking cameo appearance in the MCU film Captain America: The First Avenger (2011).[123] Matalas had always planned for Bettany to play J.I.M. besides Vision—but not Horton; various "stunt castings" were considered before Bettany suggested taking on all three roles to "really understand the corrupted files being passed down from OS systems and humans into robots, and then robots finally into humans in a visual way", and Marvel Studios approved the idea.[13] Similarly, Spader plays not only Ultron, but also the "Client" who placed a bounty on Vision's head.[28] Additional castings include Polly Frame as Fiona Guthrie;[32]: 6  Bettany's wife Jennifer Connelly as the voice of the AI D.E.E.D.E.E.,[66] having previously voiced another AI character, Karen, in the MCU film Spider-Man: Homecoming (2017);[124] and Brian Quinn as Dale, a character name that recurs for the actor whenever he appears in series for which Matalas serves as showrunner, such as 12 Monkeys, MacGyver (2016–2021), and Picard.[67] Joe Locke reprised his MCU role of Billy Maximoff from Agatha All Along in the finale of VisionQuest.[63]

Design

[edit]

Will Hughes-Jones served as the production designer for VisionQuest,[125][126] with Sarah Young as the costume designer after she was an assistant costume designer on Age of Ultron.[127][8] Hair, makeup and prosthetics designer Frances "Fran" Hounsom also worked on the series.[105]

Despite VisionQuest not using generative AI, Hughes-Jones deliberately emulated the flawed aesthetic of AI software renderings in the design of the Mindspace, including stair banisters that lead nowhere, chairs built into walls, and wonky furniture.[128] The exterior of the Mindspace appears as a crisp, white structure, echoing the color of Vision's body at the beginning of the series. For the mansion's exterior, Hughes-Jones drew inspiration from the architecture of Edinburgh—a place that has long held significance for Vision—as well as from the paintings of Edward Hopper and M. C. Escher. Hughes-Jones and his team initially envisioned the Mindspace as a house surrounded by other buildings on a busy urban street, though they ultimately decided that the mansion should stand on its own. The kitchen of the Mindspace features 40 custom-made products—each with its own unique branding—created specifically for VisionQuest, including the Vis-O-Matic vacuum cleaner, Infinidairy 2-percent milk,[103] Vision's Mustard, Firewall Hot Sauce, Mindstone Soup,[32]: 7  and Vibranium Foil, the latter being a reference to the metal alloy vibranium,[9] the material from which Vision's body is constructed.[129]

Ultron's attic in the Mindspace is filled with books, works of art, and a massive stack of 18 CRT televisions, which he uses to monitor the outside world. In this space, there is also a six-armed statue of Julius Caesar—a nod to the difficulty AI-generated imagery often has in rendering arms, legs, and fingers. Hughes-Jones noted that the attic is encircled by 12 steel columns, which enclose the space like the bars of a prison; the tops of these columns were designed to evoke Ultron's robot jaw. Furthermore, Hughes-Jones remarked, "If you look above the windows in Ultron's loft, there are some circular windows, which are the same pattern as Vision's eyes in stained glass. So, he's always watching him." The production team debated for many weeks what the view outside the Mindspace should look like, until Hughes-Jones suggested it be a screensaver reminiscent of an iconic Windows Desktop image, featuring a landscape with a blue sky, rolling green fields, and fluffy clouds,[103] which Matalas described as having "vibrant, bright, almost Mario Bros.-like colors".[32]: 7  The view from the attic, however, differs from that of the rest of the mansion, with the skyline outside appearing as a blend of New York, London, and other cities. Spader stated that this detail "spoke to the dichotomy between Ultron and the rest of the inhabitants of the house".[103]

Young approached VisionQuest with the idea that the costumes could visually represent AI. She had to recreate the White Vision costume for the series because the only two original examples remaining from WandaVision had deteriorated while in storage.[105] which was written by Matalas alongside Young. Similarly, Vision's costume reflects the silhouette of his robotic form, including a jumper with the Mind Stone in its center.[126] Hounsom developed a custom paint mixture for Bettany as White Vision and recreated details associated with Vision's previous appearances in the MCU. For Ultron's human avatar, Young developed the character's costume in collaboration with Spader, incorporating robotic design elements into the coat's seams, buttons, and belt buckle. Spader also grew a beard, which was dyed to transition from darker shades to silver and white.[105]

Tommy's costumes were designed based on the color palette of his counterpart from the comics Speed.[126] E.D.I.T.H. possessed an extensive wardrobe consisting of 21 distinct outfits—more than any other character in the series—including those of a boxer, bullfighter, priest, race car driver, field medic,[105][32]: 8  and spy;[54] a consistent color palette of red, blue, and yellow was developed to maintain continuity despite the character's frequent costume changes. Costume references for E.D.I.T.H. included Amelia Earhart, female World War II pilots, Formula One branding, and vintage aviation, all reinterpreted through the Mindspace aesthetic.[105][32]: 8  Paladin's clothing is deliberately less conspicuous than in the comics, allowing the character to blend into the crowd. His wardrobe included both tactical and street versions of his jacket.[105] J.I.M.'s red suit was inspired by the comics design as well as the suit created for The First Avenger, with that suit appearing in the background of the series, which Matalas described as "mostly a painted diving suit", so it was not featured as prominently. Clockwork designs and motifs were featured in the suit and original source code chip, which Matalas called "really fresh and fun and era appropriate" for what early attempts at artificial intelligence could be.[13]

Filming

[edit]

Principal photography began by March 14, 2025, at Pinewood Studios in London,[130] under the working title Tin Man,[131] with Byrne, Monteiro, Natali, and Matalas directing.[85] Christopher Ross, Neville Kidd, and Ben Smithard were the cinematographers.[132][133][32]: 1  Location shooting took place in and around London,[125][independent source needed] as well as at various sites across the United Kingdom.[32]: 6  Practical sets for the series' interior sequences were constructed both on sound stages and on the backlot at Pinewood Studios;[134] notable examples included the Mindspace mansion and a set spanning two streets that represented the crime-ridden city of Madripoor, the latter of which featured the Princess Bar.[126] The former set took 14 weeks to build and consisted of seven fully functional rooms (not including Ultron's attic)—kitchen, living room, dining room, entrance hall, hallway, boiler room, and movie theater—all designed to be physically connected to one another, allowing for continuous camera movement.[103]

From late March to early April 2025, filming took place at a local pub in the Chiltern Hills, England, which served as the location for The Old Selkie pub. The set dressing included the Saltire, the national flag of Scotland, displayed outside the building.[134] Other locations included Hatfield House in Hertfordshire, England, which stood in for Horton Manor; and White Waltham Airfield in Berkshire, England, used for scenes set in Paladin's jet hangar.[32]: 7  The scene in which Vision finds Horton and J.I.M. in the Mindspace took three days to film because Bettany had to constantly change his makeup for the three characters.[114] Bettany's stunt double, John Macdonald, assisted him while filming the scene, reading the other two character's lines while Bettany was filming the other character.[13] Filming wrapped on July 31, 2025,[93][134] and was completed in August,[125][independent source needed] when Matalas indicated that production spanned seven months.[41]

Post-production

[edit]

Drew Nichols, Amelia Allwarden, Jamie Gross, Christopher Gay, and McKenna Cook served as editors for the series.[32]: 1  John Haley served as visual effects supervisor, while Jonathan Barrass was the special effects supervisor. There were over 280 practical effects in the series, including magnetic rigging for Vision's flight scenes and interactive LED reflections for the Mindspace walls.[32]: 8  The Mindspace set was equipped with practical effects to represent the moments when Vision is injured in the outside world: lights flicker and emit sparks, and the walls shift, revealing boilers and machinery hidden within.[103] The production team also created a functional mechanical component—to be positioned beneath a prosthetic arm—which was capable of lighting up on camera.[105]

The second mid-credits scene of the final episode, featuring Robert Downey Jr. as Victor von Doom / Doctor Doom and Olsen reprising her role as Wanda, was excluded from press screeners given out before the episode's release.[70]

Marketing

[edit]

Footage from the series was included in a sizzle reel that was shown at Disney's upfront presentation in May 2025.[90] Bettany and Winderbaum promoted the series during a Marvel Animation and Marvel Television panel at New York Comic Con 2025, where footage was shown.[52] Mekishana Pierre and Nick Romano at Entertainment Weekly noted the "trippy setting" of a mansion in Vision's mind where he sees personifications of other AIs from the MCU, comparing this to the "reality-warping vibes" of WandaVision and Agatha All Along.[29] The appearance of Spader as Ultron in both robot and human forms was also noted by commentators.[29][30][140] Bettany and Matalas promoted the series during the 53rd Saturn Awards ceremony in March 2026,[141] and Bettany appeared at Disney's May upfront presentation to announce the premiere date and show more footage.[4]

At Disney's D23 convention in August 2026, a VisionQuest booth displayed a screen framed like a painting which showed a mysterious figure engulfed in flames. The image would glitch to show glimpses of Ultron. Commentators believed the fiery figure could be the original Human Torch.[115] Also at the convention, Feige, Bettany, and Spader promoted the series during a panel and debuted the first trailer before it was released online.[1] The trailer, which featured the song "Losing My Mind",[142] was described as "trippy" by Kirsten Chuba and Aaron Couch at The Hollywood Reporter,[143] "mind-bending" by James Hibbs for Radio Times,[144] and "as weird, if not weirder, than WandaVision" by Michael McWhertor of Polygon.[145]

A second trailer was released on October 1, 2026. Michael Walsh of Nerdist praised the trailer, calling it an "absolute banger" that presented VisionQuest as "surreal, mysterious, heartfelt, and funny", while also bringing "real stakes" for both the series' characters and the MCU as a whole.[146] Bettany promoted the series at San Diego Comic-Con Málaga 2026 on October 3, where the first 22 minutes of the premiere episode were screened.[147] Bettany further promoted VisionQuest alongside Spader, Mollica, Hampshire, and Matalas at New York Comic Con 2026 that same month, where the series' first episode was screened.[148] Also part of that convention was an immersive pop-up exhibition titled "Controlled, Altered, Deleted: The VisionQuest Collection", which was on display at Studio 525 in New York on October 9 and 10. The exhibition featured parodies of famous paintings, such as Grant Wood's American Gothic (1930) and Hopper's Nighthawks (1942), among others.[149] An episode of VisionQuest was screened at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival's third annual TV Sidebar on October 26—an event at which Bettany received the Virtuoso Award.[150]

Release

[edit]

VisionQuest had its first two episodes screened at the 2026 BFI London Film Festival on October 12,[151][152] ahead of their premiere on Disney+ on October 13;[153] the series was originally scheduled to premiere on October 14.[4] The remaining six episodes were released weekly in two-episode blocks, followed by the final two episodes releasing individually on November 3 and 10, respectively.[153] It is a part of Phase Six of the MCU.[42]

Reception

[edit]

Critical response

[edit]

On review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes, 86% of 97 critics gave the season a positive review, and the average of rated reviews was 7.30 out of 10. The critics' consensus reads, "Picking up where WandaVision left off, this series investigates the humanity beneath the robot, where the artificial becomes reality with heart, deliberate emotion, and a clear-eyed VisionQuest."[154] Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 64 out of 100 based on 25 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[155]

Audience viewership

[edit]

Luminate, which gathers viewership data from some smart televisions in the United States, informed that VisionQuest generated 639 million minutes viewed during its first ten days of streaming.[156] Nielsen Media Research, which measures streaming viewership on certain American television screens, reported that the series was the seventh most-streamed original series between October 19–25, with 490 million minutes of watch time.[157][158]

Podcast

[edit]

VisionQuest Official Podcast, a companion podcast episode for the series, was released on November 10, 2026. Hosted by Iman Vellani, who portrays Kamala Khan / Ms. Marvel in the MCU, and Frank Alvarez, it features a conversation with Bettany, Spader, Mollica, and Matalas. The episode was released on Disney+ and YouTube, with an audio-only version available on various podcast platforms.[159]

Future

[edit]

In September 2026, Matalas said he had ideas for how the story of VisionQuest could continue in future seasons,[111] while Bettany said the series was introducing a "new type" of Vision and would be a "springboard" for the character's future MCU appearances.[5] By November, there had been talks at Marvel Studios regarding the future of some of the characters from VisionQuest in the MCU.[160]
